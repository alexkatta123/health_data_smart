AI-Enabled Disease Tracking and Health Data Intelligence Platform

A statewide AI system to automate disease classification, detect emerging trends, and enable real-time decision-making in public health surveillance.




Theme: AI for Health Data and Disease Surveillance

Department:Health, Medical & Family Welfare Department, Government of Andhra Pradesh


Challenge Teaser

Strengthen Andhra Pradesh’s health surveillance network with AI.

Develop a smart platform that reads health records, classifies diseases automatically, and predicts emerging patterns — enabling faster, data-driven interventions for public health management.


Challenge Overview

Accurate and timely disease tracking is essential for effective health planning, but current data systems across Primary Health Centers (PHCs), Community Health Centers (CHCs), and district hospitals remain fragmented and inconsistent. Manual entry delays reporting and limits early detection of public health risks.


The Health, Medical & Family Welfare Department seeks to develop an AI-based Disease Tracking and Classification System that automatically reads, classifies, and analyzes health data across all facility levels. Using advanced natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning (ML), the system should interpret unstructured text (like patient symptoms, diagnosis notes, and treatment descriptions) and map them to standardized classifications such as ICD or SNOMED CT codes.


The solution will help identify emerging disease patterns across regions, improve swreporting accuracy, and strengthen preventive health measures under the Integrated Disease Surveillance Program (IDSP).




Expected Solution

Participants are invited to build an AI-driven system for disease detection and classification that includes:


Automated Data Classification: AI/NLP models that interpret patient EMRs and classify cases as communicable, non-communicable, or other diseases.


Trend Detection Engine: Identify temporal and spatial patterns across OP/IP datasets using ML and geospatial analytics.


Standardized Coding: Integration with SNOMED CT and ICD databases to ensure consistency across reporting systems.


Visualization Dashboards: Role-based dashboards for officers to monitor real-time disease trends at PHC, district, and state levels.




Learning Feedback Loop: Continuous model improvement through officer feedback and validation reports.


Integration: Ability to interface with existing state health portals and hospital systems (PHC/CHC/HMIS).





Outcomes


Improved data quality, accuracy, and timeliness of health reporting.


Automated generation of actionable disease trend insights.


Reduced manual workload for medical officers and health data entry staff.


Faster detection of regional health risks for proactive interventions.





Expected Impact


Health Department & Officials Real-time, AI-assisted monitoring of disease outbreaks and faster IDSP submissions.


Healthcare Providers Reduced manual burden, improved data consistency, and visibility into local disease dynamics.


Citizens Early detection and targeted containment of outbreaks; optimized allocation of medical resources.



Dataset Access



The following datasets (anonymized) will be provided for model development and testing:


Outpatient (OP) Records: Patient visits, symptoms, diagnoses, and treatment details.


Inpatient (IP) Records: Admission, procedures, discharge summaries, and outcomes (available for secondary and tertiary hospitals).


Disease Classification Data: SNOMED CT and ICD-coded datasets for disease tagging.


Demographic Data: Patient age, gender, and geographic location.





Proof of Concept (PoC) Scope




Implement the model for one pilot district as identified by the department.


Demonstrate classification accuracy and real-time visualization for both OP and IP records.





Proof of Concept (PoC) Success Criteria




Generation of actionable disease trend reports at hospital and district levels.


Minimum 90% accuracy in automated classification based on validation data.


Seamless integration with state dashboards for visualization and reporting.







Data Privacy and Compliance

The solution must ensure full compliance with the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023, anonymize patient information, and adhere to state health data handling protocols. Data storage and model training must occur within secure, government-approved environments.
give some work flow

Here’s a practical, end-to-end workflow you can use to design your AI-enabled disease tracking and health data intelligence platform. I’ll keep it grounded in how real public health systems operate so it’s implementable—not just theoretical.

1. Data Ingestion Layer (Collection & Integration)
Start by pulling data from all facility levels:

PHCs / CHCs / District Hospitals (OP + IP records)

Existing HMIS systems

Lab reports, prescriptions, discharge summaries

How it works:

APIs or secure batch uploads fetch structured + unstructured data

Support formats: text notes, PDFs, CSVs, EMR exports

Real-time ingestion for OP, periodic sync for IP

Key output: Unified raw health data stream

2. Data Preprocessing & Standardization
Raw hospital data will be messy—this step cleans it.

Steps:

Remove duplicates and invalid entries

Normalize medical terminology (e.g., “fever” vs “pyrexia”)

Handle missing values

Language handling (English + local languages like Telugu)

Techniques:

Rule-based cleaning + ML-based normalization

Medical dictionaries

Key output: Clean, structured dataset ready for AI models

3. NLP-Based Clinical Text Understanding
This is the core intelligence layer.

Input:

Doctor notes

Symptoms

Diagnosis text

Treatment descriptions

Process:

Named Entity Recognition (NER): Extract symptoms, diseases, drugs

Text classification models (BERT/clinical NLP models)

Context understanding (e.g., “suspected dengue” vs “confirmed dengue”)

Key output: Structured clinical entities from unstructured text

4. Disease Classification Engine
Now map extracted data into standardized systems.

Steps:

Map entities → ICD / SNOMED CT codes

Classify into:

Communicable diseases

Non-communicable diseases

Others (injuries, rare diseases)

Approach:

Hybrid model:

Rule-based mapping for known patterns

ML classifier for ambiguous cases

Target: ≥ 90% classification accuracy

5. Trend Detection & Prediction Engine
This is where public health value really shows.

Inputs:

Classified disease data

Time + location + demographics

Processes:

Time-series analysis → detect spikes

Geospatial clustering → identify hotspots

ML models:

Outbreak prediction (e.g., dengue rise)

Seasonal pattern detection

Example outputs:

“Malaria cases increased 30% in last 7 days in District X”

“Potential outbreak cluster detected”

6. Geospatial Intelligence Layer
Map diseases visually.

Features:

District / Mandal / PHC-level mapping

Heatmaps for disease spread

Risk zones (high, medium, low)

Tech stack:

GIS tools + APIs (e.g., map layers)

7. Visualization & Decision Dashboards
Different stakeholders need different views.

Dashboards:

PHC Level: Daily case summaries

District Officers: Trends, alerts, comparisons

State Officials: Statewide analytics, outbreak warnings

Key features:

Real-time charts

Drill-down filters (age, gender, disease)

Alert system (threshold-based notifications)

8. Feedback & Learning Loop
Without this, your system will stagnate.

Process:

Medical officers validate AI predictions

Corrections fed back into system

Continuous model retraining

Result:

Improves accuracy over time

Builds trust among healthcare staff

9. Integration Layer
Your system must plug into existing infrastructure.

Integrations:

HMIS systems

IDSP reporting platform

Hospital software

Method:

REST APIs

Secure data exchange protocols

10. Data Privacy & Security Layer
This is non-negotiable.

Measures:

Data anonymization (remove PII)

Encryption (in transit + at rest)

Role-based access control

Compliance with DPDP Act 2023

Deployment:

Government-approved cloud or on-prem servers

11. Output & Reporting
Final deliverables generated automatically:

Daily/weekly disease reports

Early outbreak alerts

IDSP-ready submissions

Resource planning insights (beds, medicines)

Simple Flow Summary
Data Collection → Data Cleaning → NLP Extraction → Disease Classification
→ Trend Analysis → Geospatial Mapping → Dashboard Visualization
→ Feedback Loop → Continuous Improvement
Pro Tip (What most teams miss)
Don’t over-focus on fancy AI models early. The real challenge is:

messy health data

inconsistent formats

integration with legacy systems

If those aren’t handled well, even the best ML model won’t hit 90% accuracy.
